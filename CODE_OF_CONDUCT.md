# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help.

## What we expect

- Engage with the argument, not the person making it.
- Accept that people have different levels of context. A question that seems
  obvious to you was not obvious to whoever asked it.
- Say what you mean plainly. Directness is welcome; contempt is not.
- When you are wrong, say so and move on. This project's own history is full of
  corrections; that is how it stays correct.

## What is not acceptable

- Personal attacks, insults, or demeaning comments.
- Harassment of any kind, in the issue tracker or anywhere the project is
  discussed.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any sort.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussion.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages — and whenever someone is representing the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainer through GitHub. Reports are handled
confidentially, and the person reporting is not required to confront whoever
they are reporting.

Maintainers are expected to act on reports, and to hold themselves to the same
standard. A maintainer who does not is a problem in the same way anyone else
would be.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity and repetition: a private word, a public correction, a
temporary ban from project spaces, or a permanent one. The aim is to keep the
project a place people are willing to contribute to, not to punish.
